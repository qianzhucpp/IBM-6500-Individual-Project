    2 2. Hypothesis Testing

    The professor reviewed three types of t-tests:

    • One-sample t-test: compares one sample mean with a standard value.

    • Independent-samples t-test: compares the means of two different groups.

    • Paired-samples t-test: compares two related measurements from the same people.

    If the p-value is less than .05, we reject the null hypothesis. If the p-value is greater than .05, we fail to reject the null hypothesis.

    3 3. Choosing the Right Test

    • Metric variable and one group: one-sample t-test

    • Metric variable and two groups: independent-samples t-test

    • Metric variable and more than two groups: ANOVA

    • Two categorical variables: cross-tabulation and chi-square test

    • One categorical variable compared with expected results: chi-square goodness-of-fit test

    • Several questions measuring one construct: reliability analysis and factor analysis

    For an independent-samples t-test, we should also check equal variance by using Levene’s test.

    5 5. Descriptive Statistics

    For metric or interval variables, we can use:

    • Mean

    • Median

    • Standard deviation

    • Minimum and maximum

    • Skewness and kurtosis

    For categorical variables, we use:

    • Counts

    • Percentages

    • Frequency tables

    For charts:

    • Use a bar chart for nominal or ordinal variables.

    • Use a histogram for interval or ratio variables.

    • Use box plots to compare groups.
